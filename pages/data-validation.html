Data Linkage & Validation

Cross-checking police transport reports against medical mortality registrations to ensure bulletproof data integrity.

The Challenge: Administrative Data Is Full of Hidden Friction

Public sector planning and spatial analytics often rely on administrative datasets as if they were objective ground truths. In practice, administrative data is complex, evolving, and prone to systemic gaps:

  • Scope & Definition Divergence: Police collision statistics (STATS19) capture reported crashes on public highways. Medical mortality records (ONS/Coroner ICD-10 transport codes V01–V99) record underlying cause of death regardless of location or reporting body.
  • Coroner Inquest Lags: Death registrations involving coroners often take months or years to resolve. Temporary holding codes (e.g., ICD-10 U50.9) delay final cause-of-death coding, creating historical discrepancies against real-time police stats.
  • Format & Schema Shifts: External data providers quietly update past reporting years, alter file structures, or revise reporting criteria after publication.

If an analytics system blindly ingests these datasets without cross-validation, decision-makers risk building long-term transport policies on incomplete or silently corrupted numbers.

Featured Analytical Modules

1. Cross-Source Casualty Linkage (STATS19 vs. Coroner ICD-10)

By linking police-reported collisions with medical death registrations, we reconcile structural gaps between transport reporting and medical mortality data:

  • Scope Reconciliation: Disentangles collisions occurring on private land, medical events preceding a crash (e.g., cardiac arrest prior to collision), and suicide classifications (X82) that may be miscategorised in initial reporting.
  • Coroner Lag Adjustment: Tracks historical revision curves as coroner inquests conclude, ensuring long-term mortality trendlines accurately reflect true year-of-death statistics rather than reporting lag.
  • Completeness Assessment: Measures under-reporting rates across transport modes and geographical regions by comparing health system admissions against police scene reports.

2. National Benchmark Calibration (RRCGB Alignment)

Custom spatial queries and sub-national filters should never exist in isolation. Every analytical output in our system is calibrated against official national standards:

  • Cryptographic-Level Baseline Verification: Our automated test suites run continuous verification queries comparing our pipeline outputs directly against published tables in Reported Road Casualties Great Britain (RRCGB).
  • Zero Discrepancy Guarantee: Matching national published totals down to the exact decimal gives stakeholders absolute confidence that custom LSOA spatial filters, local authority aggregations, and sub-population splits are mathematically sound.

Built on Open, Deterministic DataOps

Data validation is only as good as the infrastructure supporting it. Our validation pipeline treats data updates as continuous code integrations:

NoteThe DataOps Guarantee
  • Data Time Travel (DVC): Raw source files and intermediate datasets are version-controlled using Data Version Control. If a government dataset is quietly updated two years after initial release, we can roll the database back to any exact historical state to explain why numbers changed.
  • Automated Smoke Alarms (snapshotr): Regression tests flag unexpected shifts in row counts, spatial coverage, or indicator totals the second a dataset is updated—preventing bad data from quietly reaching production dashboards.
  • Deterministic Schema Migrations: All database schema changes and spatial lookup updates are versioned and executed programmatically, leaving a complete audit trail for external reviewers.
